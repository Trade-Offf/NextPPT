On Method

The grid system is an aid to the designer — it does not make a good design, it makes a good design possible. The freedom of the grid is the freedom from arbitrariness.

— Josef Müller-Brockmann, Grid Systems in Graphic Design, 1981

Origin · 1910s–1960s

Born of industry, raised by
typographers in Zürich.

The Swiss Style emerged from the convergence of De Stijl, Constructivism, and the Bahaus — carried forward by a generation of Swiss typographers who sought a visual language of objectivity, clarity, and structural order. It became the house style of post-war corporate identity.

Four Principles

The discipline rests on four ideas,
each older than the style itself.

01
Clarity
over decoration
Form follows function. The page exists to be read, not to be admired.
02
Rational
grid order
Every element is placed against a measured grid. Nothing drifts.
03
Asymmetric
balance
Tension is created by offset, not symmetry. Negative space is a material.
04
Objective
photography
Images document, they do not persuade. Flat, frontal, unembellished.

07 · Grid Types 07 / 10
Grid · Three Forms

A grid is a contract
between the page and the eye.

Columnar
Vertical rhythm, newsprint lineage. The newspaper's quiet ancestor.
Modular
A field of cells. Each cell a question; each cell an answer.
Baseline
Horizontal rhythm. Lines of text align like soldiers at rest.

On Silence

The empty page is not empty.
It is full of intention.

Negative space is the loudest argument the Swiss Style makes. What you leave out is what the reader believes.
